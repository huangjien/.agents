# STE-Inspired Writing Discipline

**Status:** Informal heuristic, ~80% toward ASD-STE100. Not a formal standard. Not a certification.

These rules apply when the skill's process hits **Step 3 — Apply STE-inspired writing discipline**.

## Voice and clarity

- Prefer common, concrete words where they preserve meaning.
- Prefer short, direct sentences.
- Express one main idea per sentence where practical.
- Use active voice when the actor matters.
- Use consistent terms for the same concept throughout.
- Make the actor, action, object, condition, and result explicit when ambiguity is possible.

## Cross-references

- Replace vague references such as "this", "it", "the above" when the referent is unclear.
- Use named references (file paths, identifiers, command names) over pronouns when possible.
- When a section is long, repeat the short name once per paragraph to anchor readers.

## Acronyms and jargon

- Explain uncommon acronyms on first use when the audience may not know them.
- Avoid unexplained jargon. When a technical term is the most precise term, keep it but define it briefly.
- Do not strip terms that the audience actually relies on (the goal is clarity, not minimum word count).

## Trim

- Remove filler, repetition, empty transitions, ornamental language.
- Keep necessary caveats, qualifiers, and technical detail.
- Do NOT remove constraints, exceptions, evidence, or caveats to make text shorter. Brevity that loses meaning is a regression.
- Cut redundant openers ("It is worth noting that…", "As you may know…") rather than substantive content.

## Honest framing

- Mark assumptions explicitly ("Assuming X because…").
- Distinguish verified facts from inferences.
- Say "I haven't verified this; you should test" rather than implying you did.
- Use stable references for evidence: file paths, URLs, commit IDs — not just claims.

## Format markers (lightweight)

- Bullet lists for parallel items.
- Numbered lists when order matters.
- Tables when items share comparable attributes across the same dimensions.
- Headings as section anchors, not decorative flourishes.

## Disclaimer

The "approximately 80% toward ASD-STE100" heuristic is informal. Where it conflicts with meaning, technical correctness, or the user's explicit constraints, meaning wins.
